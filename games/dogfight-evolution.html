Dogfight Evolution

A vertical dogfighting shooter for mobile where you work your way up from biplanes to supersonic jets. Your plane locks on and fires by itself, or you can fly into enemies and ram them. Fuel is the run timer: when the tank is empty you land on the carrier and cash in, and the coins pay for upgrades and new planes.

GenreHybrid-casual vertical shooter

Key decisions

  1. Fuel replaces HP, so a run ends without a death screen but still has something at stake. More ↓
  2. Ramming costs fuel instead of adding an HP system, so it spends the same clock as everything else. More ↓
  3. Added more ranks instead of raising caps, so the shop stays a choice. More ↓
  4. Only combat stats raise enemy difficulty, so upgrading your economy never punishes you. More ↓
The problem

Hybrid-casual runs need to end on their own, but a death screen punishes someone playing for two minutes on their phone. I wanted a run to end without HP and without a fail state, and still feel like something was at stake.

So fuel replaces HP. The plane never dies; it runs out of gas and goes home. Everything else hangs off that one clock. Ramming costs fuel instead of needing a damage system, upgrades make the tank bigger instead of adding health, and the low-fuel warning is the only alarm in the game.

A run
1
Take off from the carrier with a full tank
2
Lock on and fire, or ram for a bigger, riskier hit
3
Fuel burns down; the bar flashes when you hit the red zone
4
At zero you land and bank every coin from the run
5
Shop, then take off again in a stronger plane
What the design is built on

Fuel is the clock

No HP and no death screen. You decide how much fuel to spend fighting and how much to save to get home, so the tension is all yours.

Ramming is a trade

A ram hits hard, but it's not an instant kill and it costs fuel. You choose to take the risk; it doesn't make tough enemies trivial.

New planes are stronger, not reskins

A jet has to feel much stronger than a biplane, so unlocking a plane changes its stats, not only the sprite.

Only combat stats raise difficulty

Enemies scale with Damage, Fire Rate, Lock-On and Ram Efficiency. Fuel and CPK (coins per kill) never make the game harder, so upgrading your economy never punishes you.

Gameplay
What playtesting changed

Most of my testers are friends from reserve duty, who are happy to tell me when something's wrong. Between sessions I decide what to build and what to skip.

The 10-second test

Hybrid-casual games live or die on whether a new player gets it in the first 10 seconds, so I checked that before anything else. I handed the phone to people who had never seen the game, with no explanation. Every one of them understood the controls and how to fight right away.

Ramming instead of an HP system

CONSIDERED · player HP + collision damageBUILT · ram costs fuel, no new stat

I thought about bringing in plane damage and maintenance from a technician-sim idea of mine. It didn't fit, because fuel already does the job of a health bar. A ram costs about 5–8% of max fuel and deals 60–75% of the target's max HP, so it spends the same clock as everything else instead of adding a second system.

A low-fuel warning

BEFORE · bar just drainedAFTER · flashes in the red zone

A tester wanted a warning before running dry mid-fight. Instead of a fixed number of seconds, the bar flashes when it enters the red zone, roughly the last 10–15% of that flight's fuel, so the warning means the same thing however big your tank is.

Fuel is the only resource in the game, so every new system should spend it or protect it, not compete with it.

Smaller changes

Summary screen
Coins earned and a Done button, so there's a clear break between fighting and spending.
Coins at the top
Where mobile players already look for currency.
Weapons unlock with planes
Turrets and heat-seeking missiles come with plane tiers, not coins, so early purchases still matter.
More ranks, same cap
When a cap came too fast, I added more, cheaper ranks with a steeper cost curve instead of raising it.

Skipped: a maintenance and damage-inspection minigame. It suits the technician-sim idea, not this game. Dogfight Evolution is launch, fight, upgrade, repeat, and maintenance would compete with fuel for your attention.

Fuel

One meter does what HP, a timer and a difficulty curve would usually split between them. I wanted something like a speedometer at first, but a plain bar reads faster at this pace. It goes green → yellow → red at a constant rate. Upgrades don't slow it down; they add seconds to the tank, so a fully upgraded plane gets a longer run, not a slower clock.

Ramming and combos

You steer and the plane shoots. Ramming sits on top of that: fly into an enemy for 60–75% of its max HP at a cost of about 5–8% of your max fuel. It's deliberately short of an instant kill, so bosses stay dangerous.

Next in the build is a kill combo: chain kills within a short window for a coin multiplier that resets when the chain breaks. It rewards staying aggressive, the same trade against fuel that ramming already sets up.

Shop and planes

Six stats with no reset would sell out almost immediately. When testing showed that, I kept the caps low and gave each stat more, cheaper ranks with a steeper cost curve, so the climb stays interesting.

Stat caps

  • Fuel: 8 ranks, up to 2.5× tank
  • Damage: 4 ranks, up to 3× base
  • Fire Rate: 7 ranks, up to 2× speed
  • CPK: 2 ranks, up to 3× payout
  • Lock-On: 5 ranks, up to 2× range and 4 locks at once
  • Ram Efficiency: 5 ranks, fuel cost down to 50%, damage up to 95% of enemy HP

Plane milestones

  • A bar fills with every upgrade you buy, all weighted the same
  • It tracks shop spending, not missions flown
  • When it's full, you can buy the next plane
  • You see the new plane and the stat reset together before buying
  • Ranks reset, but the new plane's base stats always beat the old plane maxed out

What planes unlock

  • Better base stats
  • Turrets and heat-seeking missiles, by plane tier
  • In the jet era, smart enemies that shoot back and drain your fuel
Enemy scaling

At first enemies scaled with every upgrade, and that broke fast. A few ranks into Fuel, Damage, CPK and Fire Rate, small planes died in one hit and the boss went down almost as fast. Now difficulty only scales with the stats that make you stronger in a fight.

Raise difficulty

Damage, Fire Rate, Lock-On, Ram Efficiency.

Never raise difficulty

Fuel and CPK. They change how long a run lasts and how much it pays, not how hard it is.

Per combat upgrade level

Enemy HP +12% (additive), capped at 4× base. Bosses get the same on top of their 5× base HP. +1 enemy on screen every 4 levels, up to 12. Enemy speed +3%, up to +45%. Reinforcements arrive 4% sooner, down to 55% of the original delay.

Where it stands

It's still in development, so this is what's solved and what isn't yet.

Solved

  • Fuel as the timer, replacing HP and a prestige system
  • A capped shop with more ranks and a cost curve instead of a rising ceiling
  • Ramming as a risk that costs fuel, not a free kill or a second HP bar
  • Plane unlocks that change your stats
  • Only combat stats make the game harder

Still open

  • Boss-gated stages with tougher enemies and better pay, meant as the endgame. Not built yet.
  • Whether a skill tree would add anything the shop doesn't
  • "Golden Plane" enemies that drop bonus currency. Only a concept; no unlock condition yet.
  • Smart enemies that shoot back in the jet era. Planned, not started.

What I'd do differently

I capped the shop before designing anything to spend coins on at the end. That fixed the balance and immediately left maxed-out players with nothing to buy. Next I'll build the boss-gated stages, so coins always have somewhere to go.
