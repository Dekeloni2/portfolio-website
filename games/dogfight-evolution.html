Dogfight Evolution

A hybrid-casual vertical dogfighting shooter — climb from lumbering biplanes to supersonic jets. Auto-lock onto enemy planes and unload, or close the distance and ram them out of the sky. Fuel is your run timer: burn it fighting, or save enough to make it back to the carrier and cash in. Every kill funds the next upgrade; every upgrade earns a stronger plane.

Design Pillars
01

Fuel Is the Player's Clock

No player HP, no death screen. Every run is bounded by a draining fuel bar instead — you decide how much of it to spend fighting versus saving to get home. The tension is entirely self-imposed.

02

Ramming Rewards Aggression, at a Cost

Closing the gap and ramming an enemy hits hard, but it isn't a free instant-kill and it isn't free fuel either. It's a deliberate trade a player opts into, not a system that trivializes tougher enemies.

03

New Planes Are Power Spikes, Not Skins

A fighter jet has to feel meaningfully stronger than a biplane. Plane unlocks change the stat line, not just the sprite — progression is felt, not just seen.

04

Difficulty Tracks Combat Power, Not Utility

Enemies scale off Damage, Fire Rate, Lock-On, and Ram Efficiency only. Fuel and CPK — the pacing and reward stats — never make the game harder, so grinding economy never accidentally punishes the player.

Core Loop

One run, one clock. The whole meta-loop hangs off a single resource: fuel goes down, coins go up, and everything you buy comes back around to change how long you can stay in the fight.

1
Launch from the carrier with a full fuel tank
2
Auto-lock and fire on enemies in range — or close in and ram for a bigger, riskier hit
3
Fuel burns as your run clock — a flashing warning hits once you're in the red zone
4
Return to the carrier before it hits zero and bank every coin from kills you chained
5
Spend at the shop — then launch again in a stronger plane
Core Actions
🎯

Auto-Lock & Fire

Lock onto enemy planes in range and fire automatically. The core damage dealer, scaled entirely by shop upgrades — Damage, Fire Rate, and how many locks you can hold at once.

💥

Ram

Fly straight into an enemy for roughly 60–75% of its max HP in one hit, at a fuel cost of about 5–8% of your tank. Strong, but never a guaranteed kill — boss-tier enemies stay dangerous.

⛽

Fuel Management

The bar drains green → yellow → red at a constant rate and doubles as your run timer. Upgrades raise the tank's max duration in seconds — later planes simply carry more fuel.

🔥

Combo Chain

Chaining kills within a short window builds a currency multiplier; let the chain lapse and it resets. Rewards staying aggressive instead of picking off planes one at a time.

Gameplay — In Motion

What starting a run actually looks like: the takeoff sequence that doubles as the game's loading screen, then straight into the dogfight — auto-lock, fire, fuel draining, ramming when it's worth the risk.

How I Designed It

The 60-second version — the reasoning behind Dogfight Evolution, from the core problem to what I'd change next. The full breakdown is below; this is the map.

01 · Problem

A run needs a clock, not a death

Hybrid-casual sessions need to end on their own, but a death screen punishes a mobile player for a short session game. How do you close a run without HP, without a fail state, and without it feeling like nothing was at stake?

02 · Design Decision

Fuel replaces HP entirely

The plane never dies — it just runs out of gas and heads home. Every later system hangs off that single clock: ramming costs fuel instead of adding a damage system, upgrades extend the clock instead of granting more health, and the low-fuel warning is the game's only urgency cue.

03 · Prototype / Test

Built the loop, then handed it to testers

Once the carrier → dogfight → summary → shop loop was playable end to end, I put it in front of testers. Feedback flagged a missing low-fuel warning, a wish for ramming instead of a new HP system, and — once a few stats were maxed — the game just got too easy.

04 · Result

Cap the shop, split what scales difficulty

Gave every stat a hard rank ceiling instead of open-ended scaling, and split the six shop stats into "combat" (Damage, Fire Rate, Lock-On, Ram Efficiency) and "economy" (Fuel, CPK) — only combat stats now scale enemy difficulty. See the full iteration ↓

05 · What I'd Change Next

Give a maxed-out economy somewhere to go

Capping the shop solved the balance problem but created a new one: a player who maxes everything has nowhere left to spend currency. Boss-gated stage tiers — tougher enemies that pay more — are the planned endgame sink, still unbuilt.

Playtesting & Iteration

My testers are largely miluim (reserve duty) peers — a built-in, opinionated group who'll actually tell me when something's wrong. Between sessions I triage: build what serves the loop, defer what doesn't.

✓

Passed the 10-second clarity test

Before touching balance or systems, I checked the thing hybrid-casual lives or dies on: can a player tell what's going on in the first 10 seconds? I handed the phone to people who'd never seen the game, cold, no explanation — every single one instantly understood the gameplay, the controls, and how to fight. If that read takes longer than a few seconds, nothing else about the design matters; this is the result everything else in this breakdown was built on top of.

Ramming replaced a separate HP system

CONSIDERED · player HP + collision damage SHIPPED · fuel-cost ram, no new stat

I'd considered carrying a plane-damage/maintenance idea over from a separate technician-sim project of mine. It didn't fit — Dogfight Evolution already has one resource doing the job of a health bar. Ramming instead costs fuel (~5–8% of max) and deals 60–75% of the target's max HP, so it's a trade against the same clock the whole game already runs on, not a second system to track.

Added a low-fuel flash warning

BEFORE · bar just drained, no cue AFTER · flashes at the red zone

A tester asked for a clearer signal before running dry mid-fight. Rather than pick an arbitrary second count, the flash triggers at the same threshold as the bar's red zone — roughly the last 10–15% of that flight's max fuel — so the warning always means the same thing regardless of how upgraded the tank is.

Both changes came from the same instinct: fuel is the only resource in the game, so every new system should spend or protect it — never compete with it.

Same build cycle — UX & economy pass

Smaller decisions cleared alongside the bigger ones:

Post-dogfight summary screen
Coins earned + a Done button before returning to the shop — a clear beat between "fighting" and "spending."
Coins shown at the top of screen
Kept currency in the conventional mobile HUD position players already expect.
Automatic weapons progression-gated
Turrets and heat-seeking missiles unlock with plane tier, not pure shop currency — keeps early purchases meaningful.
Granular ranks over raised caps
When a stat's cap felt reachable too fast, I added more, cheaper ranks with a steeper cost curve instead of raising the ceiling.

Scoped out: a plane-maintenance/damage-inspection mini-game, carried over from a separate technician-sim concept of mine. It's a good mechanic for that game, not this one — Dogfight Evolution's identity is "launch, fight, upgrade, repeat," and a maintenance layer would compete with fuel for the player's attention instead of feeding the same loop.

Fuel — The Player's Clock

A single meter does the job HP, a timer, and a difficulty curve would normally split across three systems. It's a bar, not a dial — I'd wanted something closer to a speedometer, but a straightforward bar reads faster and fits the hybrid-casual pace better.

The fill gradients green → yellow → red as it drains at a constant rate. Upgrades don't change the drain — they extend the tank's total seconds, so a fully-upgraded plane simply gets a longer run, not a slower clock. See it draining in the Combat clip ↑

Combat — Ramming and the Combo Chain

Auto-lock and fire is the baseline — the player steers, the plane shoots. Ramming is the layer on top: close the distance and fly into an enemy for 60–75% of its max HP in one hit, at a cost of roughly 5–8% of max fuel. Deliberately short of an instant kill, so boss-tier enemies stay a real threat instead of becoming a one-touch trivializer.

A decaying kill-combo multiplier is next in the build queue: chain kills within a short window to boost the coins earned, and let the chain lapse and it resets. It's meant to reward staying aggressive rather than picking planes off one at a time — the same aggression-vs-fuel trade-off ramming already sets up. See a ram land in the Combat clip ↑

Shop & Progression — Capped Stats, Milestone Planes

A finite shop with six stats and no reset would run out of things to sell almost immediately. Instead of raising caps when that happened in testing, every stat got more, cheaper ranks and a steeper cost curve — the ceiling stays low, the climb to it stays interesting.

Stat Caps

  • Fuel — 8 ranks, up to 2.5× tank capacity
  • Damage — 4 ranks, up to 3× base damage
  • Fire Rate — 7 ranks, up to 2× attack speed
  • CPK — 2 ranks, up to 3× base payout
  • Lock-On — 5 ranks, up to 2× range, 4 simultaneous locks
  • Ram Efficiency — 5 ranks, fuel cost floor 50%, damage ceiling 95% of enemy HP

Plane Milestones

  • Milestone bar fills from total upgrades purchased, weighted equally
  • Not tied to missions flown — pure shop investment
  • New plane bought with currency once the bar fills
  • Reward and stat reset shown together, before purchase
  • Ranks reset, but the new plane's base stats always beat the old plane's maxed ranks

Unlocks Tied to Planes

  • New planes change the stat line — power, not a reskin
  • Turrets and heat-seeking missiles unlock progressively by plane tier
  • Jet-era planes introduce smart enemies that return fire, pulling down your fuel
Enemy Scaling — Difficulty Tracks Combat, Not Economy

The first version scaled enemies off total upgrades, full stop — and it broke fast. Once Fuel, Damage, CPK, and Fire Rate were all a few ranks in, small planes died in one hit and the boss went down almost as fast. The fix was to scale difficulty only off the stats that actually make the player stronger in a fight.

Counts

Scales Enemy Difficulty

Damage, Fire Rate, Lock-On, Ram Efficiency — the four stats that raise combat power.

Doesn't

Never Scales Difficulty

Fuel and CPK — pacing and reward stats. Upgrading them changes how long or how lucrative a run is, never how hard it is.

Scaling curve, per combat upgrade level:

  • →Enemy HP: additive +12% per level, capped at 4× base HP
  • →Bosses receive that same scaling on top of their 5× base HP
  • →Concurrent enemies: +1 every 4 upgrade levels, capped at 12
  • →Enemy speed: +3% per level, capped at +45%
  • →Reinforcement delay: −4% per level, capped at 55% of the original interval
Where This Stands

Still in development, so this is a working postmortem rather than a finished one — an honest read on what's solved and what's still an open problem.

Solved

  • Fuel-as-timer resource loop, replacing HP and a prestige system entirely
  • Capped, granular upgrade economy tied to a cost curve instead of a rising ceiling
  • Ramming as a fuel-costed risk/reward tool, not a free instant-kill or a second HP system
  • Milestone-gated plane unlocks that change the stat line, not just the sprite
  • Enemy difficulty scaling split so only combat stats — not economy stats — make the game harder

Still Open

  • Boss-gated stage tiers (tougher enemies, better payout) — the planned endgame sink, not yet built
  • Whether a skill-tree layer adds anything the capped shop doesn't already cover
  • "Golden Plane" bonus-currency enemies — concept only, unlock condition undecided
  • Smart enemy AI and return fire for the jet era — planned, not started

The lesson so far

Capping the shop before designing an endgame sink was the wrong order — I fixed a balance problem and immediately created a "nothing left to buy" problem. Next pass: build the boss-gated stage tiers first, so a maxed-out economy always has somewhere to go.
