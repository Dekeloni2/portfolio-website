Rest in Profit

A cozy-macabre mobile game — a dark parody of Disco Zoo. You're an aspiring necromancer: dig up undead minions from graveyards, deploy them in a dungeon, and let them defend against looting "heroes" while they earn you coins. Equal parts collection game and idle dungeon-defense, welded together by one economy.

Design Pillars
01

Cozy-Macabre

Warm and charming about death — a friendly-Grim-Reaper tone, not grim horror and not a joke. The mood drives the art, the tutorial voice, and every bit of copy.

02

An Underused Hook

Disco Zoo's tap-to-reveal dig loop is one of mobile's most addictive mechanics — and strangely underexplored. The whole game is built to give that hook a world worth digging in.

03

Two Loops, One Economy

A collection loop (dig) and a combat loop (defend) feed each other through a single currency — coins. Defend to earn, spend to dig and upgrade, dig to grow stronger. Neither loop goes vestigial.

04

Systems as the Star

Monster roles, a trade-off relic loadout, two-lane progression, and a tuned idle economy. It's designed to be a systems-design showcase as much as a game.

The Core Loop

The whole game is one self-feeding loop. Every currency earned in one half is spent to grow the other.

1
Dig a graveyard — reveal undead by uncovering their pattern
2
Uncover & collect — duplicates level a monster up
3
Deploy into the dungeon by role
4
Defend against looting heroes — earn coins
5
Upgrade & dig deeper — repeat
Core Systems

⛏️ Grave Digging

Enter a 5×5 site with a mana budget and reveal connected patterns to unearth undead. Rarer monsters use fewer tiles — so they're harder to find, and running one tile short creates the "spend to close the gap" tension.

💀 Collection & Leveling

Find duplicates to level a monster: each level raises its income, a milestone unlocks its signature ability, and maxing it out is its own reward. Set-collection, not merge.

⚔️ Idle Dungeon Defense

A moving-unit auto-battler. Monsters self-organize into a formation by role and fight waves of invading heroes while you're away. No lose screen — a soft wall, not a fail state.

🪙 Two-Currency Economy

Coins (from defense) drive power and access; mana is the per-dig budget; Soul Gems are premium time-and-convenience. The loops interlock through coins without collapsing into one grind.

📿 Relics & the Reliquary

Equip a capped loadout from a pool of trade-off relics. Rarity means downside severity, not just power — and a one-legendary cap forces a real build decision.

🌙 Idle Earner

Monsters keep earning while you're offline, capped to a fair window, with income that scales with how deep a wave you can hold. Come back to a meaningful pile, not an exploit.

How I Designed It

The 60-second version — the reasoning behind Rest in Profit, from the core problem to what's next. The full breakdown is below; this is the map.

01 · Problem

Make an idle game with real depth

Disco Zoo's dig loop is brilliant but underused, and idle collection games go flat the moment you've collected everything. Could I fuse that dig hook to an idle dungeon-defense so the two loops feed each other and the game stays deep past "collection complete"?

02 · Design Decision

One economy, layered systems

One currency — coins — ties collection to combat: defend to earn, spend to dig and upgrade, dig to grow stronger. On top sits a systems layer (monster roles, a trade-off relic loadout, two-lane progression) that turns "collect monsters" into "build and tune a dungeon."

03 · Prototype / Test

Prove the hook before building on it

I built the dig loop first and validated it in isolation — ~25 real dig runs confirmed the near-miss tension was firing (I missed the rarest finds most, exactly as intended) before I let combat depend on it.

04 · Result

A working loop — and a real diagnosis

The loop closes end-to-end. When combat felt broken — a maxed unit folding at wave 4 — I diagnosed it as a positioning-AI gap, not a balance problem, and the same team held ~10 waves further. See the diagnosis ↓

05 · What's Next

Content breadth & an endgame

Wire the designed relic pool into combat; build biomes (each area its own monsters, relics and challenge); and add prestige — Timeless monsters, Disco-Zoo style — so digging stays meaningful past completion.

Playtesting & Iteration

The most useful design work on this project came from reading a result correctly instead of reacting to it. Two examples — one a diagnosis, one a guardrail.

A "balance" failure that was really a positioning gap

BEFORE · folds at wave 4 AFTER · holds to ~wave 14

A fully-leveled squad was collapsing at wave 4, which looked like a numbers problem. It wasn't — my ranged damage-dealer was standing in the melee getting deleted. The fix was a role-based positioning AI (Frontline / Damage / Support, each with a standoff distance derived from its attack range), not a stat change. Same stats, same monsters — it held about ten waves further. The lesson: diagnose the root cause before you touch the numbers.

Keeping the idle economy honest

RISK · uncapped AFK farming FIX · two guardrails

An idle earner where monsters never die can print money forever. I keep it honest with two levers: offline income is time-capped (8 hours), and income scales with wave depth — so farming easy content is low-value and there's no "park and get rich." The raw payout number was never the question; the caps are.

Both cases came from the same instinct: a surprising number is a question, not a verdict. Find out why before you change anything.

Gate access gently, gate power steeply

A guiding rule for the economy that keeps the game cozy while still having a grind:

Digging = linear cost
Expedition entry rises gently (Disco-Zoo style), so collecting never walls you.
Upgrades = exponential cost
Raw combat power is the real grind and the central coin sink.
Premium = time, not power
Soul Gems buy convenience and cosmetics — never stats — so it stays fair.

Honest about scope: the full relic pool, prestige, and additional biomes are designed but not yet built — the loop above is what's playable today. Knowing what to build now versus what to leave on paper is part of the job.

Deep Dive

Everything below is the full breakdown — the dig loop, the role-based defense, the economy, and the relic system that ties the build layer together. The summary above is the whole argument in short; go as deep as you want from here.

The Dig Loop — In Motion

The dig site is the piece I built and tuned first. You enter a 5×5 graveyard with a limited mana budget and tap tiles to uncover the connected pattern of a buried monster — Disco Zoo's shape-reveal, reframed as grave-robbing.

Everything present in a dig is telegraphed, rarer monsters occupy fewer tiles (so they're harder to pin down), and the whole thing is tuned so you often come up one tile short — the exact moment the game offers a premium nudge. That near-miss tension is the loop's engine, and I validated it with real play before building anything on top of it.

The Dungeon Defense — Role-Based Auto-Battler

Placed monsters aren't static — they move and fight in real time, and they organize themselves. Each monster carries a role that drives a self-forming formation, so the tactical decision is which roles you bring, not micromanaging positions on a phone screen.

Frontline
Wants the front and soaks damage. If it's ever behind the pack, it speeds up until it's back on the wall.
Damage
Hangs back and only advances when its target leaves range — so a ranged unit holds deep, and a melee unit kites just behind the wall.
Support
Holds the middle, between the wall and the damage line.

Heroes raid the dungeon rather than racing an exit, peeling off to smash graves and chests along the way. There's no lose screen — a downed monster can always be revived to keep grinding, so failure is a soft wall that nudges you back to digging and upgrading, not a punishment.

Progression & Economy

Two power axes, kept in separate lanes so each stays legible — and an economy tuned so the two coin sinks never starve each other.

Duplicate leveling

  • Find dupes to level a monster
  • Each level raises its income
  • A milestone unlocks its ability
  • Maxing it is a capstone moment

Coin upgrades

  • Spend coins on Attack & Health
  • Separate tracks, per monster
  • Linear stat gain, exponential cost
  • Capped — a known power ceiling

The currencies

  • Coins — power & access
  • Mana — the per-dig budget
  • Soul Gems — premium, time only
  • Offline income, capped & fair

Dupes deepen what a monster does (income + identity); coins raise its numbers (combat power). Keeping those lanes apart means a player always knows what each currency is for — and why the next dig or the next upgrade matters.

Relics & the Reliquary

The build layer — and the deepest systems-design work on the project. You collect relics and equip a capped loadout from the pool, so every relic on is a relic you chose over another. Two rules keep the choices meaningful:

Rule 01

Rarity = downside severity

Commons carry a small cost, Rares a sharper trade-off, Epics are clean power, and Legendaries redefine a build. Rarity tells you how spicy the trade is, not just how strong.

Rule 02

Every relic stands alone

A relic has to justify its slot on its own — synergy is a bonus between two independently-good relics, never a prerequisite. And you can equip only one Legendary, so it anchors the build.

Built From Scratch — Solo

Everything here is mine: the design, the C# systems, the pixel art, and the tooling around it. To make a systems-heavy game maintainable solo, I built my own Unity editor tools — custom inspectors and windows for authoring dungeons, defining monsters and their level curves, cropping sprite sheets, resetting player progress, and simulating offline time to test idle income. The tooling is why one person can keep this many interlocking systems balanced.
