Why I'm Building This

Call of Duty Zombies is a big part of how I became a game designer. The early games ran on a loop that was almost embarrassingly simple — survive the wave, spend your points, come back stronger — and that simplicity is exactly what made it so good. Somewhere along the way the mode got bloated and complex, and the clean loop I fell in love with got buried under systems. Undead Invasion is my attempt to answer a specific question: can I faithfully recreate that experience in my own design, and keep it as tight as it used to be?

My main reference is Call of Duty: Black Ops III — widely considered the high-water mark for Zombies — so most of my research and design decisions trace back to what it got right. The goal isn't to out-feature it; it's to understand it well enough to rebuild the feel from first principles.

It's also a deliberately full-stack piece for me, and my first serious Unreal project: I'm authoring the audio, the animation, the writing, and the wave systems myself. So the page below is organized around what each discipline is proving rather than a feature checklist.

Design Pillars
01

Craft You Can Hear and Feel

Every gunshot, footstep, and undead groan is built from scratch, and every animation is referenced from real footage. The moment-to-moment feel is the point — not borrowed asset-pack polish.

02

Faithful to the Simple Loop

Shoot, survive, spend, come back stronger. I'm rebuilding the classic Zombies loop from first principles — Black Ops III as the reference — and defending its simplicity instead of piling systems on top of it.

03

Reasons to Keep Fighting

Four survivors, each written with their own backstory and motivation, and one Entity watching over the run. The narrative sits underneath the shooting instead of pausing it.

04

Waves With Authored Moments

Waves aren't just "more enemies." Scripted wave events change what a given round means, so the run has beats and surprises rather than a flat difficulty ramp.

The Round Loop

The core loop is intentionally classic — its job is to be instantly legible so the craft and the wave design carry the experience.

1
Survive the wave — hold ground against the undead
2
Earn points from kills and clears
3
Spend on Perk-a-Colas, weapons, and the map
4
Face the next wave — and whatever event it triggers
What It Showcases
🔊

Sound Design

Every sound in the game is created from scratch — weapons, the undead, ambience, and UI. The audio is treated as a design layer, not a garnish: it's how the game communicates threat, feedback, and weight.

🎞️

Animation

Animations are built from real-life reference — existing footage and video I record myself — so movement, recoil, and reloads read as grounded and intentional rather than stiff or generic.

📖

Narrative

Four playable characters, each with their own story and motivation, plus a single Entity watching over the player. The writing gives the survival run stakes and a throughline beyond the score.

🌊

Wave Management

The system that governs how the undead arrive — spawn pacing, count, and pressure per round. Tuning this is what keeps each wave tense without tipping into unfair.

⚡

Wave Events

Scripted "what happens on wave X" moments layered on top of the base loop — special rounds and surprises that give the run authored beats and break the monotony of a flat ramp.

🥤

Perk-a-Colas

Buyable perk machines in the Zombies tradition — the spend side of the loop. They turn earned points into permanent-for-the-run power and give players build decisions between waves.

Cast & The Entity

The Brief I Set Myself

The 60-second version of what this project is for. It's a learning piece in an established genre — the value is in how it's built, not a novel core mechanic.

01 · Goal

Rebuild classic Zombies, in Unreal

Recreate the simple, tight Zombies loop I grew up on — Black Ops III as the standard — while learning Unreal on a genre I understand cold, so the unknowns are all engine and craft.

02 · Approach

Own every discipline

Build the audio, animation, narrative, and wave systems myself instead of leaning on packs — so the piece proves range, not just that I can assemble a shooter.

03 · What It Proves

Faithful recreation & restraint

That I can study a beloved design, rebuild its feel, and have the discipline to keep it simple — plus the hidden craft (sound, feel, authored moments) and a third engine alongside my Unity and MonoGame work.
